About Me

Somewhere between a 2 AM pager alert and a clean rollout, I found the work I actually love: making sure systems stay up, and stay boring, in the best possible way.

It started at Accenture, where I walked in as a fresher and left, three years later, having touched almost every layer of a production system: Linux boxes, Docker Swarm clusters, and the CI/CD pipelines that either save your evening or ruin it. Somewhere in that stretch, "DevOps Engineer" stopped being a job title on my badge and started being the way I actually thought about problems, not "does it work," but "will it still work at 3 AM, under load, when someone least expects it to fail."

That question is what pulled me deeper into AWS and Kubernetes, building deployment pipelines with rolling, blue-green, and canary strategies, delivered GitOps-style through ArgoCD. It's also what led one of my biggest projects yet: migrating production microservices off Docker Swarm and onto AWS EKS, piece by piece, without anyone downstream noticing the ground had shifted beneath them. Prometheus, Grafana, ELK, and Dynatrace became less like tools and more like a second set of eyes, the ones that tell you something's wrong before a single user does.

Today I'm a Consultant at Deloitte, working alongside product owners and operations teams on change management, incident resolution, and continuous delivery. And increasingly, I find myself drawn toward Site Reliability Engineering, because the instinct behind SRE is the same one that's driven me from the start: fewer surprises, faster recovery, and systems people can trust without thinking about them at all. That's the next chapter I'm looking to write.
